Chapter 1: Introduction

1.1 Background of the Study

Retail hardware stores occupy a uniquely important position in the Philippine economy. They supply the raw materials, tools, and construction supplies that local households, small contractors, and community infrastructure projects depend on daily. Unlike supermarkets or convenience chains, hardware retailers typically operate as independent, family-owned businesses — often single-location, owner-managed, and serving a walk-in customer base within a specific barangay or municipality. Their operational efficiency directly determines how quickly a community can build, repair, and maintain its homes and infrastructure.

The scale of this sector is substantial. As of end-2023, the Philippines had 1,241,766 micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs), accounting for 99.6% of all firms and employing 6,364,367 workers, or 66.8% of the total workforce (Asian Development Bank [ADB], 2024). Wholesale and retail trade consistently holds the highest share of MSMEs at 48.6%, and 82.1% of these enterprises are located in the provinces outside the National Capital Region (ADB, 2024). Mom-and-pop retail stores alone contribute an estimated ₱1.12 trillion to ₱1.46 trillion annually to the national retail economy, underscoring their role as a critical component of Philippine commerce (San Juan, 2025). More than 99% of businesses in the Philippines are MSMEs, contributing roughly 40% to gross domestic product (Cruz, 2026).

Kirby's Hardware Trading, located along Gungon Street in Santa Maria, Bulacan, is one such enterprise. The store stocks a broad catalog spanning nine categories — tools, hardware, electrical, plumbing, paint, garden, building supplies, fasteners, and safety equipment — and serves a mix of walk-in retail buyers and small-scale contractors. As with most Philippine hardware retailers of comparable size, the store has traditionally relied on manual processes: handwritten stock ledgers, carbon-copy sales receipts, whiteboard price lists, and month-end arithmetic done by hand.

These manual practices, while adequate during the store's earliest years, have become increasingly difficult to sustain. A growing product catalog (now exceeding three hundred SKUs, many with size and brand variations) has made accurate stock tracking impractical to maintain by hand. Research on retail MSMEs confirms that inventory management without proper information systems potentially causes recording errors, reporting delays, and data discrepancies that affect managerial decision-making (Pratiwi et al., 2026). A growing volume of daily transactions has made end-of-day reconciliation error-prone. And a growing base of repeat customers has made the absence of any customer-record system an active business limitation — the owner cannot identify his best buyers, cannot measure their spending patterns, and cannot target any kind of loyalty effort.

1.1.1 The Shift in Retail Technology

Over the past decade, point-of-sale (POS) and inventory management systems have become standard equipment in retail businesses worldwide. In larger urban markets, integrated POS platforms are sold as turnkey solutions bundled with dedicated hardware — barcode scanners, receipt printers, cash drawers, and touch-screen terminals. These systems deliver real-time inventory tracking, automatic sales reporting, and customer relationship management as core features. Modern POS systems automate inventory tracking, generate sales reports, and offer user-friendly interfaces that require minimal training, supporting product variants such as size and color (Stripe, 2025). POS systems help retailers track what is selling and what is not, understand store performance over time, manage inventory more efficiently, and offer better service to customers (Budhiraja, 2025).

For small neighborhood retailers, however, the traditional hardware-based POS model presents three substantial barriers:

  1. Capital cost. A complete commercial POS terminal typically costs between ₱25,000 and ₱80,000 — a significant investment for a single-location store operating on thin retail margins.
  2. Connectivity dependency. Many commercial POS systems assume a stable broadband connection. In areas where internet service is intermittent — a common condition in provincial and semi-urban Philippine communities — cloud-dependent POS terminals become unusable during outages, precisely when a manual fallback is most disruptive. One major issue to resolve is the expensive and slow internet connection in the country (Philippine Institute for Development Studies [PIDS], 2026).
  3. Operational rigidity. Commercial POS software is often designed for high-volume retail chains and does not accommodate the workflows of a small owner-managed store — for example, selling by per-size or per-brand stock slices, or issuing handwritten receipts with a store footer.

At the same time, three technology shifts have made a fundamentally different approach feasible:

  • Universal browser availability. Every laptop, tablet, and smartphone manufactured in the last eight years ships with a modern web browser capable of running complex client-side applications — eliminating the need for dedicated hardware. Modern POS systems have mobile apps accessible on smartphones and tablets, offering flexibility and ease of use (Stripe, 2025).
  • Serverless cloud platforms. Serverless architecture has emerged as a transformative cloud computing model, offering small and medium enterprises scalable, cost-efficient alternatives to traditional infrastructure, with the market projected to grow at a 22.2% compound annual growth rate driven by pay-per-use pricing and automatic scaling (Preety & Sharma, 2025). This pricing structure significantly reduces operational costs, making serverless computing particularly attractive for startups and SMEs (Research and Markets, 2025).
  • Client-side offline storage. Modern browser APIs allow web applications to function fully offline — reading cached data, queuing writes, and syncing automatically when connectivity returns. Cloud Firestore provides iOS, Android, and Web SDKs with offline data access and real-time data synchronization (Google, 2017). IndexedDB is a persistent store that lets applications save and retrieve JavaScript objects even without network connectivity (Microsoft, 2024b). Service workers make applications more reliable by enabling them to work even when the user's device is offline or has an intermittent network connection (Microsoft, 2024a).

These three shifts — universal browsers, serverless infrastructure, and offline-capable clients — together make it possible to build a POS system that costs nearly nothing to run, works on hardware the store already owns, and remains fully functional through internet outages.

1.1.2 The Case for an AI-Assisted POS

Beyond replacing manual workflows with digital ones, the study also addresses a secondary gap in the small-retail segment: business intelligence. A store owner with a paper ledger can look up what happened yesterday, but has no practical way to answer questions like:

  • "Which products sold best this month?"
  • "Are my sales trending up or down?"
  • "What should I restock before the weekend?"
  • "How much will I likely sell next week?"
  • "Who are my top customers?"

Answering these questions manually requires hours of arithmetic and produces results that are already stale by the time they are computed. Commercial POS systems answer them, but only after the store has invested in the hardware and connectivity those systems demand.

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) and statistical forecasting have made it feasible to embed business intelligence directly into a low-cost POS platform. Generative AI drives a wave of AI-enabled applications that support business functions from customer service and regulatory compliance to content generation and decision support (Gartner, 2026). Generative artificial intelligence has had a transformative impact on business, with an estimated 35% of projected $297.9 billion AI software spending targeting GenAI by 2027, up from 8% in 2023 (EurekAlert, 2025). Organizations are using GenAI to generate price quotes, review contract compliance, and create interactive digital manuals (Nasdaq, 2025). The application of natural language processing (NLP) technologies represents an attractive innovation that can help chatbots communicate with users more precisely, empathetically, and individually (IEEE, 2025a). A statistical forecasting engine can project the next seven days of revenue and rank products by restock urgency. Neither capability requires the store to purchase analytics software or hire a data analyst.

This study is therefore motivated by a simple premise: a small Philippine hardware retailer should be able to run a fully digital, AI-assisted point-of-sale and inventory operation on the hardware it already owns, with no recurring software cost, and without losing business continuity when the internet goes down.

1.1.3 Rationale for the Study

Kirby's Hardware Trading was selected as the case study for this development project for four reasons:

  1. Representative of a larger market segment. The store's operational profile — a single-location, owner-managed hardware retailer with a mid-sized SKU catalog and a walk-in customer base — is broadly shared by thousands of similar Philippine retail businesses. Mom-and-pop stores are typically classified as MSMEs, which account for more than 99% of businesses in the Philippines (Cruz, 2026).
  2. Willingness to adopt technology. The store owner has expressed explicit interest in digitizing operations and has been actively involved in defining the workflows the proposed system must support. While 77% of Filipino MSMEs are eager to adopt digital tools, only 16% currently use them (Carandang, 2026).
  3. Sufficient complexity to validate the design. The store's catalog includes products with size, brand, and weight variations — a nontrivial modeling requirement that a system lacking variant support would fail to represent correctly.
  4. Real operational pain. The store's current manual processes generate measurable losses in time, inaccuracy, and missed opportunity. MSMEs in emerging economies continue to suffer from inept inventory management, demand forecasting, and logistics coordination, especially in rural and semi-urban setups (IEEE, 2025b).

The proposed system — Kirby's Hardware POS and Inventory Management System — was developed in direct response to these operational realities. It is a browser-based, offline-capable, AI-assisted POS platform designed from the ground up for the constraints of a small Philippine hardware retailer: low capital budget, intermittent connectivity, owner-operated staffing, and a diverse product catalog.

1.2 Problem Statement

Kirby's Hardware Trading currently operates using fully manual business processes. These processes, while functional in a low-volume context, produce a set of interrelated operational problems that constrain the store's ability to grow, to make informed decisions, and to serve its customers reliably. The specific problems the proposed system intends to address are enumerated below.

1.2.1 Inventory Management Problems

P1 — Manual stock tracking drifts from reality. Inventory quantities are maintained in a handwritten ledger and updated by hand after each sale. Research on retail MSMEs shows that businesses still applying manual inventory and financial recording using notebooks frequently experience discrepancies between purchase and sales data (Pratiwi et al., 2026). Over time, small omissions and arithmetic errors accumulate, so the ledger no longer reflects what is actually on the shelf.

P2 — Low-stock detection is reactive, not proactive. There is no mechanism that alerts the owner when a product falls below a safe threshold. Manual inventory systems become resource-heavy and expensive due to the amount of time and general lack of efficiency involved in inputting data, monitoring for accuracy, reconciling against financials, and correcting errors (Fishbowl, 2022). Keeping track of inventory by hand makes it difficult to forecast changes in supply and demand, which can result in product shortages and overstock (Fishbowl, 2022).

P3 — Variant-heavy products are difficult to track accurately. Many products (hammers, PVC pipes, paint, wiring) are stocked in multiple sizes, weights, or brands. Modern POS systems help retailers manage product variants such as size and color (Stripe, 2025). A system lacking this capability cannot accurately represent the store's inventory.

1.2.2 Sales and Transaction Problems

P4 — Sales recording is manual and error-prone. Each transaction requires the cashier to compute the line totals by hand or with a calculator, apply the tax rate, and write a receipt on a carbon-copy pad. Cashier arithmetic errors, miskeyed prices, and illegible handwriting all contribute to inaccurate daily totals. Small businesses often face challenges such as limited staff, tight budgets and the need to manually track inventory, which can lead to inefficiencies and lost revenue (Stripe, 2025).

P5 — Transaction history is inaccessible. Locating a specific sale — by date, by customer, by receipt number, or by product — requires physically searching through months of paper receipts. In practice, this is not done. Transactions effectively vanish from the store's historical record after a few months.

P6 — Receipt records are vulnerable to loss. Paper receipts are stored in a filing cabinet or box. Water damage, fire, insect damage, or simple misplacement can destroy the only copy of a transaction record. Modern POS systems provide cloud access so businesses can manage everything in one place (Stripe, 2025).

1.2.3 Reporting and Business Intelligence Problems

P7 — Period-based reporting is impractical. Generating a sales report for a specific week, month, product category, or customer requires the owner to manually tally dozens or hundreds of receipts. The effort involved means such reports are generated rarely and inconsistently, if at all. Modern POS systems generate sales reports and offer user-friendly interfaces that require minimal training (Stripe, 2025).

P8 — No visibility into sales trends. The owner has no way to see whether sales are growing or declining, which products are accelerating or slowing, or what time of day the store does its busiest business. A POS system's biggest advantage is the ability to track store performance with real-time data including total sales by day, week, or month, top-selling products, peak sales hours, and trends over time (Budhiraja, 2025).

P9 — No forecast capability. The owner cannot project next week's likely revenue, and has no data on which to base a cash-flow plan. Research has shown that 82% of small businesses fail due to cash flow problems, and many organizations lack a robust cash flow forecasting strategy due to limited data, technology constraints, and uncertainty (Creative Planning, 2025). SMEs operate under fundamentally different constraints: limited historical data, basic invoicing systems, incomplete record-keeping, and minimal resources for financial analysis (Financial Management System for SMEs, n.d.).

1.2.4 Customer Relationship Problems

P10 — Repeat customers are invisible. A handful of regular buyers — particularly small contractors — account for a disproportionate share of revenue, but the store has no record of who they are. A good POS system lets businesses build customer profiles that include name, contact information, order history, and preferred products, enabling a more personalized shopping experience (Budhiraja, 2025).

P11 — Order history cannot be attributed to a customer. Even when the owner recognizes a returning customer by face, there is no way to look up that customer's purchase history to prepare for the visit or to make a targeted recommendation. Customer profiles allow businesses to send promotions, offer loyalty rewards, or reach out to regulars when a product they love is back in stock (Budhiraja, 2025).

1.2.5 Operational Oversight Problems

P12 — No accountability for staff actions. When more than one person works in the store, the owner has no reliable way to determine who recorded a specific sale, who adjusted a stock count, who changed a price, or who deleted a record. SMEs that build data privacy and audit capabilities into their processes from the start — and design evidence-ready systems to meet legal standards — will stand out as platforms and regulators tighten (Carandang, 2026).

P13 — Settings and prices cannot be centrally managed. Store information (name, address, receipt footer, tax rate, GCash credentials) is either memorized or written on a note. There is no single source of truth, and no way to propagate a change (e.g., a new tax rate) across all points of use. Modern POS systems integrate with accounting and e-commerce tools and provide cloud access so businesses can manage everything in one place (Stripe, 2025).

1.2.6 Technology-Access Problems

P14 — Commercial POS systems are unaffordable for the store's scale. Existing off-the-shelf POS solutions are priced for high-volume retail chains and require dedicated hardware. While 77% of Filipino MSMEs are eager to adopt digital tools, only 16% currently use them — mostly due to lack of skills, perceived complexity, and affordability (Carandang, 2026). Serverless computing reduces operational costs and lowers the barrier to entry for businesses looking to leverage cloud-based solutions (Research and Markets, 2025).

P15 — Commercial POS systems fail during internet outages. Cloud-dependent POS terminals become unusable when the connection drops — a common occurrence in the store's location. One major issue to resolve is the expensive and slow internet connection in the country (PIDS, 2026). A POS system that stops working during an outage is worse than a paper receipt book, because it also loses whatever transaction was in progress. Cloud Firestore enables offline data access via a powerful, on-device database that allows the app to function smoothly even when users lose connectivity (Google, 2017). Service workers make applications more reliable by enabling them to work even when the user's device is offline or has an intermittent network connection (Microsoft, 2024a).

Together, these fifteen problems define the specific deficiencies the proposed system is designed to eliminate.

1.3 Objectives of the System

1.3.1 General Objective

To design, develop, and deploy a browser-based, offline-capable, AI-assisted Point-of-Sale and Inventory Management System for Kirby's Hardware Trading that replaces the store's manual business processes with a digital workflow, and that provides the store owner with accurate, real-time visibility into sales, inventory, and customer activity — without requiring any dedicated hardware purchase or recurring software subscription.

1.3.2 Specific Objectives

To achieve the general objective, the system aims to accomplish the following specific goals:

  1. Digitize the point-of-sale workflow. Provide an interactive cart-based POS interface that replaces handwritten receipts with structured digital order records, computes tax and totals automatically, and generates a printable receipt with configurable store header and footer.
  2. Enforce atomic stock deduction at checkout. Use transactional database operations to guarantee that stock is decremented if and only if an order is successfully recorded — eliminating the stock drift caused by manual subtractions.
  3. Support variant-rich product catalogs. Model products with size, brand, weight, and other option dimensions using a unified variant matrix, so that each distinct variant maintains its own stock count and optional price.
  4. Automate low-stock and out-of-stock detection. Continuously compare each product's stock against its configured minimum threshold and surface low-stock and out-of-stock items through a notifications panel, a dashboard widget, and a dedicated restock recommendation list.
  5. Provide searchable order history. Replace the physical receipt archive with a filterable, searchable order table that lists every transaction, opens a detail modal for any order, and supports filtering by date range and status.
  6. Build a customer database with automatic aggregation. Maintain customer records that automatically accumulate order count and lifetime spend as sales are recorded, enabling the owner to identify top buyers and understand repeat-purchase patterns.
  7. Generate one-click reports. Provide a Reports module that produces Sales, Inventory, Customer, and Financial reports on demand, exportable as CSV or as print-ready PDF, filtered by user-selected periods including custom date ranges.
  8. Deliver real-time business dashboards. Render live KPI cards and interactive charts that update without page reload as new sales and inventory changes occur.
  9. Provide a statistical sales forecasting engine. Implement a pure-Python forecasting module that scores four candidate statistical models via rolling-origin cross-validation, selects the best performer, and returns a 7-day revenue forecast with 95% prediction intervals.
  10. Provide AI-assisted natural-language querying. Integrate a hybrid AI assistant that routes structured questions to deterministic local handlers and forwards open-ended POS-relevant questions to an external LLM, with strict isolation of provider errors from the user interface.
  11. Enforce role-based access control. Support two distinct user roles — Administrator and Cashier — with the administrator granted full access and the cashier restricted to a defined subset of operational features, enforced both at the UI and at the backend API.
  12. Maintain a complete audit trail. Record every sale, login, inventory change, settings update, and user-management action in an immutable activity log that displays owner-friendly labels, supports filtering and export, and provides per-user activity summaries for staff oversight.
  13. Preserve full functionality through internet outages. Guarantee that the POS workflow, receipt generation, and inventory lookup remain fully operational when the network is unavailable, by queuing failed writes locally and replaying them automatically when connectivity returns.
  14. Deliver a fully responsive user interface. Ensure the system is usable on desktop, tablet, and smartphone browsers without modification, so that the store can operate on whatever device is available without purchasing specialized hardware.
  15. Minimize operating cost. Deploy the system entirely on free-tier or near-free serverless infrastructure so that the store's total recurring cost remains within the free tier of those platforms under typical usage. Serverless architectures offer potential cost savings of 47–62% for variable workloads (Preety & Sharma, 2025).

1.4 Scope and Limitations

1.4.1 Scope

The proposed system covers the following functional areas:

Point of Sale. A complete cart-based transaction workflow supporting cash and GCash payment methods, per-line quantity adjustment, variant selection, live tax and total calculation, cash-change calculation, GCash reference number validation, and printable receipt generation.

Inventory Management. Full product catalog CRUD with support for products ranging from zero to two option dimensions, per-variant stock and price, product image upload with automatic client-side resizing, category management, low-stock and out-of-stock detection, and CSV export.

Orders. A searchable, filterable order history with detail modals showing receipt number, customer, phone, payment method, GCash reference, and per-line item breakdown.

Customers. Customer record management with automatic accumulation of order count and lifetime spend; search across name, email, and phone.

Reports. Sales, Inventory, Customer, and Financial reports with period selection and CSV / PDF export.

Dashboard and Analytics. Live KPI cards with comparison deltas; interactive charts for revenue trend, top-selling products, category distribution, and hourly sales heatmap.

Audit Trail. Full activity log with filters, active-user panel, per-user activity summary, and CSV export.

User Management. Administrator-only user creation, editing, password reset dispatch, deactivation, reactivation, and deletion — with admin password re-authentication for sensitive operations and a maximum of two administrator accounts.

Settings. Store information, receipt footer, tax rate, and GCash credentials configuration, with live propagation to all open clients.

AI Assistant. Natural-language chat interface with local intent classification, deterministic handlers for structured queries, and external LLM fallback for open-ended questions. The AI system prompt forbids inventing numbers and sends only aggregate snapshots to the LLM.

Statistical Forecasting. A pure-Python sales forecasting engine that scores four candidate models via rolling-origin CV and returns a 7-day revenue forecast with 95% prediction intervals.

Offline Support. A service-worker-based app shell cache and an application-level pending-sale queue so that the POS remains fully operational during internet outages.

Authentication. Firebase Authentication with Firebase ID token verification on every backend request; role-based access control enforced both client-side and server-side.

1.4.2 Limitations

The system does not, and is not intended to, address the following:

L1 — Barcode scanning. The current version does not support barcode or QR-code scanning for product lookup. Products must be located manually via category browsing or text search.

L2 — Multiple store locations. The system is designed for a single-location store. It does not support inventory transfer between branches, consolidated multi-store reporting, or per-store user assignments.

L3 — Supplier and purchase-order management. Supplier names are recorded as free-text fields on product records, but the system does not manage supplier catalogs, generate purchase orders, or track inbound stock orders.

L4 — Full accounting integration. The system produces a Financial Report with revenue and tax totals, but does not maintain a general ledger and is not a substitute for proper bookkeeping or an accredited accounting system for BIR compliance purposes.

L5 — Native mobile applications. The system is accessible only through a web browser. There is no dedicated iOS or Android application. The browser-based interface is fully responsive and can be installed as a Progressive Web App, but the study does not deliver separately-distributed native apps.

L6 — Server-side rendering or SEO. The application is a client-rendered single-page interface. It is not intended to be crawled, indexed, or exposed to the public internet — all pages require authentication.

L7 — Hardware peripherals beyond the browser. The system does not integrate with cash drawers, thermal receipt printers, customer-facing display screens, or card payment terminals. Receipts are printed via the browser's native print dialog.

L8 — Payment gateway integration. For GCash, the system displays a static QR code configured from the store's GCash number and requires the cashier to record the customer's 13-digit reference number. It does not perform an automated API lookup to verify the reference number against GCash's systems.

L9 — Offline installation without prior online access. The service worker caches the application shell on first online load. A device that has never opened the app while online cannot install and open it purely offline; it must connect at least once to bootstrap.

L10 — High-availability guarantees. The system relies on free-tier serverless infrastructure. Under abnormal usage, the Firestore free-tier daily read quota can be exhausted, causing backend endpoints to return HTTP 503 for the remainder of the quota period. The system degrades gracefully but does not eliminate the ceiling.

L11 — Forecasting accuracy under sparse data. The statistical forecasting engine requires a minimum of three nonzero days to produce output, and its accuracy is bounded by the quality of the historical data. A store that has been operating for only a few weeks will see forecasts with very wide confidence intervals. Research confirms that limited historical data is a persistent challenge for SME cash flow forecasting (ICAEW, 2025).

L12 — AI answers for out-of-scope questions. The AI assistant is designed for POS-relevant questions about the store's own data. It will decline to answer questions unrelated to the store's operations and will not perform general-knowledge tasks, creative writing, or coding assistance.

L13 — Multi-tenant operation. The system is deployed for a single store. Running multiple independent stores on the same deployment would require additional tenancy modeling that is not part of this study.

L14 — Language coverage beyond English and Tagalog. The NLP intent classifier recognizes English and Tagalog / Taglish queries. Other Philippine languages are not explicitly supported.

L15 — Historical data migration. The system does not include a tool for importing historical sales or inventory records from paper ledgers or from other software.

1.5 Significance of the System

The proposed system delivers value to four distinct groups of stakeholders. Each group benefits in a specific, measurable way.

1.5.1 For the Store Owner

The store owner is the primary beneficiary and the individual most directly affected by the shift from manual to digital operations.

  • Real-time operational visibility. The owner can see today's sales, today's order count, current stock levels, and active customer counts from the dashboard at any moment. A POS system's biggest advantage is the ability to track store performance with real-time data (Budhiraja, 2025).
  • Data-driven decision making. Sales trends, top-selling products, category performance, and hourly heatmaps provide the evidence needed to make informed decisions about pricing, purchasing, staffing, and promotion. Accurately forecasting retail sales is essential for effective business operations and strategic decision-making (Slathia et al., 2025).
  • Proactive restock management. The system flags low-stock and out-of-stock items before they impact sales, and its restock recommendation list ranks items by urgency based on observed 30-day sales velocity. A POS system keeps track of inventory in real time and gives access to reports that show what's in stock, what's running low, and reorder points for each product (Budhiraja, 2025).
  • Forecasting for cash-flow planning. The 7-day revenue forecast gives the owner a data-backed estimate of expected sales. Research has shown that 82% of small businesses fail due to cash flow problems, and many lack a robust forecasting strategy (Creative Planning, 2025).
  • Complete audit trail. Every action is recorded. SMEs that build audit capabilities into their processes from the start will stand out as platforms and regulators tighten (Carandang, 2026).
  • Zero recurring software cost. The system runs entirely on free-tier serverless infrastructure. Serverless architectures offer potential cost savings of 47–62% for variable workloads compared to traditional VMs (Preety & Sharma, 2025).
  • No specialized hardware purchase. The system runs on any laptop, tablet, or smartphone the store already owns. Modern POS systems have apps accessible on mobile devices such as smartphones and tablets (Stripe, 2025).

1.5.2 For Store Staff (Cashiers)

Cashiers are the primary day-to-day users of the system and see direct improvements in their workflow.

  • Faster checkout. Automatic price lookup, tax computation, and total calculation eliminate manual arithmetic. A user-friendly POS interface makes it easier to onboard employees quickly (Stripe, 2025).
  • Fewer mistakes. Stock validation at checkout prevents overselling. Modern POS systems solve problems by automating inventory tracking and generating sales reports (Stripe, 2025).
  • Continuous operation during outages. The system remains fully functional when the internet drops. Service workers make applications more reliable by enabling them to work even when the device is offline or has an intermittent network connection (Microsoft, 2024a).
  • Searchable product catalog. Finding a product for a customer is a matter of typing a few characters into the search bar or filtering by category.
  • Clear role boundaries. The cashier role is scoped to the operations the cashier is expected to perform. Role-based access control provides a framework for managing user permissions and controlling access to resources based on predefined roles (Zenodo, 2025).

1.5.3 For Store Customers

Customers are indirect beneficiaries — they benefit from the improvements the system delivers to the store's speed and accuracy.

  • Faster service. Reduced checkout time translates directly into shorter wait times. A POS system provides fast and reliable transactions that mean shorter queue times and more satisfied customers (MagicPay, 2026).
  • Accurate pricing. The catalog is the single source of truth for prices. Customers are never charged a price that differs from what the store intends to charge.
  • Reliable stock information. Stock counts are accurate to the transaction level, so a customer asking for an item is less likely to be told it is available when it is not.
  • Better customer recognition. Because the system maintains a customer database and tracks order history, repeat customers receive more informed service. A good POS system helps provide a more personalized shopping experience and allows businesses to send promotions, offer loyalty rewards, or reach out to regulars when a product they love is back in stock (Budhiraja, 2025).

1.5.4 For the Academic Community

As a capstone development project, the study contributes to the academic and technical community in several ways:

  • Reference implementation for serverless retail systems. The system demonstrates a fully functional POS and inventory management platform built entirely on free-tier serverless infrastructure. Serverless architecture has emerged as a transformative cloud computing model offering SMEs scalable, cost-efficient alternatives to traditional infrastructure (Preety & Sharma, 2025).
  • Offline-first web application design. The architecture illustrates a practical pattern for delivering offline-capable web applications. Service workers allow developers to implement customized caching strategies that optimize both performance and reliability (Zenodo, n.d.).
  • Hybrid NLP + LLM query routing. The AI assistant's design — deterministic local intent classification for structured queries, LLM fallback for open-ended questions, and strict isolation of provider errors — provides a reusable pattern for embedding AI into cost-sensitive business applications. NLP technologies can help chatbots communicate with users more precisely, empathetically, and individually (IEEE, 2025a).
  • Pure-Python statistical forecasting. The forecasting module demonstrates a lightweight, dependency-free implementation of rolling-origin cross-validation and OLS prediction intervals. Rolling forward cross-validation accounts for the temporal ordering of time series data (Kagalwala, 2023).
  • Reproducible deployment. The system is deployed on publicly accessible, freely replicable infrastructure. Anyone can fork the repository, configure their own credentials, and have a working POS system running in under an hour.

In summary, the proposed system addresses a genuine operational gap for small Philippine hardware retailers: it delivers the capabilities of a commercial POS platform — real-time inventory, automatic reporting, customer tracking, and forecasting — without the cost, hardware dependency, or connectivity requirement that makes commercial systems inaccessible to this segment.
